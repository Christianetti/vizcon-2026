Reach and currency

Two ways to measure how music connects us.

Reach — the direction

Chart-weeks: one song, on one country's top 100, for one week. Every market weighs the same, so Iceland counts like India. It maps where music goes.

Currency — the intensity

Streams: what people actually played. Big markets weigh more, because more people pressed play. It measures how strongly it landed.

Direction and intensity. Put together, they trace the shape of how the world listens to itself — where the connections run, and how loud each one is.

Neighbourhoods

Most countries don't trade with the world. They trade with next door.

Hong Kong sends 97% of its exports to just three places. Czechia sends 76% to Slovakia. India sends 74% to Pakistan.

Below the superpowers, music trade follows language, borders and history — and a few corridors run genuinely both ways. Argentina and Spain trade almost perfectly evenly: 1,976 chart-weeks out, 1,887 back.

Concentration

Some countries are one person.

SAINt JHN is 100% of Guyana's music exports. Emilia is 78% of Bulgaria's. Kygo is 40% of Norway's.

Meanwhile the United States spreads its exports across 2,845 artists, and its biggest star accounts for under 3%.

It's petro-state logic applied to culture: spectacular output, and everything resting on one person.

The turn

The world is quietly going local — and that's the good news.

In 2017, 17% of the average country's chart was homegrown. By 2025 it was 45%.

Over the same years the top five exporters' share of world trade fell from 78% to 65%. The UK lost 43% of its export volume. India grew 236×.

Nobody stopped listening across borders. More countries simply started making music worth exporting.

Methodology

Source

Spotify Charts Daily (Kaggle: gonzalopezgil/spotify-charts-daily-updated), aggregated to a weekly top 100 per country. 100 countries and territories appear as artist origins; 70 have a Spotify chart of their own, and only those 70 can run a measurable trade balance.

Period

2017–2025, nine complete years. 2016 is excluded throughout: it contains only 4,706 domestic chart-weeks against 270,171 in 2017, and including it would fabricate a growth curve.

The two units

Reach = chart-weeks. One song, on one country's top 100, for one week. Every market counts equally.
Currency = streams. Population-weighted by construction, so larger markets contribute more.

Definitions

An export is a chart-week earned by an artist outside their country of origin. A trade surplus means a country's artists earned more chart-weeks abroad than foreign artists earned on its home chart.

Artist origin

Resolved from Wikipedia, Wikidata, MusicBrainz, Kaggle and LLM-assisted research. Dual-nationality artists count as local in both countries, with export credit split equally.

Grouping

Ward hierarchical clustering on 13 standardised metrics, Euclidean distance (cophenetic correlation 0.96). The United States is held out as an acknowledged outlier — its nearest neighbour sits 10.6 units away where typical pairs sit 1–2.5 apart. Revenue was excluded from clustering and used afterwards as an independent check.

Limitations

Chart start dates vary by country, and the number of charts grew from 56 in 2017 to 70 by 2022 — growth figures are inflated accordingly, and slide five offers a market-adjusted view. Artist-origin assignment is imperfect for relocated and dual-nationality artists. Puerto Rico and Hong Kong are territories, not sovereign states. Streaming skews younger and more urban than the general population.
