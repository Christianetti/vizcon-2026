The Paradox: Two Ways to Go Global

Here's something wonderful: English and Latin music connect the world in completely different ways.

English-language songs spread widest - they show up in the most countries simultaneously. Think of it as broadcasting to everywhere at once.

Latin songs last longest - once they land on a chart, they stay for weeks and weeks. Their median longevity is 30 weeks, compared to just 11 for English tracks.

Two strategies, both connecting millions of listeners across borders. There's no single recipe for global music. There's room for everyone.

Every Country Plays a Role

We found that countries fall into fascinating archetypes based on how they share and receive music:

🌍 Global Broadcasters

US, UK, Canada, Australia

These nations export music everywhere. The US alone sent songs to 65+ countries every single year. Their music is the shared soundtrack of the world. But here's the beautiful twist: they import plenty too. The US chart is 27% foreign.

🏠 Self-Sufficient Scenes

Japan, South Korea, Turkey

These countries have thriving local music cultures. Japan's chart is over 80% homegrown. South Korea exports massively (K-pop!) while keeping its domestic charts mostly local. They prove you can be globally influential AND locally rooted.

🌉 Regional Bridges

Colombia, Mexico, Sweden

Colombia doesn't try to reach every country. Instead, it dominates Latin America with extraordinary intensity. Swedish producers punch way above their weight, bridging Nordic and global pop. These bridge nations connect their regions to the wider world.

The Latin Secret Sauce

Why did Latin music explode onto the global stage? We found three ingredients that set it apart:

1. Collaboration Culture

Latin artists collaborate at a staggering rate. 72% of charting Latin songs are collaborations, compared to roughly 40% for English-language tracks. Every collab introduces Artist A's fans to Artist B, multiplying reach exponentially. It's community over competition.

2. Extraordinary Longevity

Once a Latin song charts, it stays. The median Latin track holds its chart position for 30 weeks. English tracks average 11. This stickiness compounds over time, building massive cumulative chart-weeks even when spread across fewer countries.

3. Concentrated Star Power

Latin music concentrates around superstars who become cultural icons. Bad Bunny alone accounts for a huge share of Puerto Rico's exports. This concentration creates a gravitational pull: fans follow the artist into the entire genre.

Methodology & Data

This analysis uses Spotify Charts data (weekly Top 200) across 70 countries from January 2016 through June 2025. "Foreign chart-weeks" counts each week a song appears on a chart outside its artist's home country. Artist origin is determined by the country most associated with the artist's career and early catalog.

Longevity measures how many consecutive weeks a song remains on a foreign chart. Spread measures the number of distinct countries where a song charted. Collaboration rate is the percentage of charting songs with multiple credited artists.
