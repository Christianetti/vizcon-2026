We looked at 3 million chart-weeks across 100 countries over 9 years on Spotify. Here is what we found: the world is already singing together.

The Song That Went Everywhere

Blinding Lights spent 7,051 weeks on foreign charts across 67 countries.

Think about that! One song from Canada became the soundtrack of commutes, parties, and late nights in 67 different countries. We really are listening to the same things, just in different time zones.

Every year from 2016 to 2025, the most globally traveled song came from a different country. The UK, the US, Canada, Australia, Puerto Rico... the stage is truly global now.

    The Artist Who Changed the Game

    Bad Bunny dominated global charts for 5 of the last 9 years.

    Latin artists have topped the "most traveled artist" chart 8 out of 9 years. Let that sink in! Not English-language pop stars. Latin artists.

    Bad Bunny alone accumulated over 70,000 foreign chart-weeks. He made the world sing in Spanish whether you understood the lyrics or not. The feeling? Universal.

      The Beautiful Paradox

      English goes widest. Latin stays longest.

      Anglophone songs spread to 55+ countries on average. Spanish-language hits reach fewer borders but stay on charts for weeks and weeks. Two completely different superpowers, both connecting millions of people.

      It is not a competition. It is a beautiful complementarity: breadth vs. depth, and together they cover the whole world.

      Three Ways Countries Listen

      Every country falls into one of three music personalities.

      Exporters send music outward. The US, UK, South Korea, and Puerto Rico produce hits that travel the globe.

      Open markets welcome foreign music with open arms. Countries like UAE, Singapore, and Luxembourg have 90%+ foreign music on charts.

      Local champions keep their own music on top. Japan, India, Turkey, and Brazil have thriving domestic scenes where local artists dominate.

      Methodology

      Data source: Spotify Charts (weekly Top 200) for 100 countries, covering January 2016 through June 2025. Approximately 3 million chart-weeks analyzed.

      Key definitions: A "foreign chart-week" occurs when a song appears on the Top 200 of a country different from the artist's origin. Artist origins were determined by Spotify metadata and manual verification for ambiguous cases.

      Metrics: Export weeks (total foreign chart-weeks), longevity (median weeks on a single foreign chart), spread (median number of countries reached), and collaboration rate (% of exported songs featuring multiple artists).

      Limitations: Spotify chart availability varies by country and start date. Some smaller markets have data starting later than 2016. Artist origin assignment is imperfect for dual-nationality or relocated artists.
